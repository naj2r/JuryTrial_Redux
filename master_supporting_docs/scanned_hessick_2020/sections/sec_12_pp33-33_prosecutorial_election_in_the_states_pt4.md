Finally, an underappreciated aspect in the discussion of elected 
prosecutors is that about ten percent of local prosecutors resigned their office 
since the latest election. This is more common for those districts that held an 
election in 2014 (about 13 percent), rather than 2016 (about five percent), 
presumably because there was more time to prompt the need for an 
appointment.  
We unfortunately do not know the reason for each appointment, nor did 
we systematically collect this information. When we did record a reason for 
appointment, a common reason was that the prosecutor had been elected or 
appointed as a judge.149 This is consistent with a recent report by the Cato 
Institute, which found that more than 38 percent of current federal judges 
are former prosecutors.150 It is also consistent with Jed Shugerman’s work on 
the “prosecutor politician.”151 Shugerman hypothesizes that the prosecutor’s 
office emerged in the last century or so as a stepping stone for higher office.152
