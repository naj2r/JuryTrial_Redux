2.2. The Model with Asymmetric Information
With perfect information the ﬁrst best occurs; high-quality prosecutors, who
are more likely to be successful in the courtroom, should be retained, but
low-quality prosecutors should be replaced. With asymmetric information,
voters no longer know the type of prosecutor in ofﬁce though they know the
probability that the prosecutor is high type. Given that there is a bonus for
being retained, even a civic-minded prosecutor’s payoff no longer coincides
with the voter and thus with asymmetric information about quality, all types
of prosecutors may try to signal they are of a high type in order to ensure
re-election. We are interested in understanding how such signals distort the
outcomes in the criminal justice system and if there is a remedy.
Before the incentives of the prosecutors are addressed, recall that for
cases where θ > θL welfare maximization requires that both high-quality
and low-quality prosecutors go to trial, while for cases where θ ≤θH both
types should plea bargain. Only if θL ≥θ > θH does optimal behavior
differ—high-quality prosecutors should take the case to trial while low-
quality prosecutors should plea bargain. Since the former is more successful
in court only her surplus from trial (the difference between the expected
sentence and the sanction that can be achieved through plea bargaining)
justiﬁes the court costs. Suppose the quality of the prosecutor is unobserv-
able to the voting population. In that case, we would like to investigate if

3. Prosecutor Evaluation
The question becomes; How does this incentive to misrepresent affect the
behavior of the prosecutor when she is of low quality and when she is of
high quality? Prosecutors may try to signal their quality by taking observable
actions and the voters, who do not have access to information on her true
type, may observe these actions and form beliefs about the prosecutor to
make their retention decision. We shall analyze the resultant signaling game
in this section (while also commenting on what the related screening game
would look like) and in the following section look at an alternate scenario
where voters screen prosecutors on the basis of multiple evaluation criteria.
3.1. Sentence Length
One way in which the voting public or appointment ofﬁcials may assess the
quality of the prosecutor is to look at the length of the sentences she is able
to obtain. She may achieve sanctions through plea bargaining or through
successes in the courtroom. Over the course of her term many cases with
differing amounts of evidence come before her for a decision. Thus, the
aggregate sanction imposed over the course of her term can potentially be
used to assess her quality.
