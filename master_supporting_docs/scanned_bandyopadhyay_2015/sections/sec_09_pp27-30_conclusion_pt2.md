A.2. Conviction Rate
Now suppose the aggregate sentence obtained is used as a metric to evalu-
ate the prosecutor. Again, suppose the prosecutor is retained if and only if
r ≥r e (r e >rH). A prosecutor can achieve this by either choosing a lower
cutoff, τq, greater than θq (since these cases have relatively low probabilities
of success at trial) and by selecting a higher cutoff, Tq, greater thanθq (since
these cases have relatively high probabilities of success). As before, for any
given r e there are numerous combinations of cutoffs ⟨τq, Tq⟩which achieve
the needed level, but now an increase in τq increases rq and, consequently,
corresponds to a lower value of Tq.
Also, as with the aggregate sentence length metric, there are numer-
ous combinations that achieves r e. Increasing τq increases utility by B(θ) −
p q(θ)s + C since plea bargains arise in place of trials, while the correspond-
ing decrease in Tq affects utility by p q(θ)s −C −B(θ) for each case changed
since now plea bargains are replaced by trials. The magnitude of the shift in
each cutoff depends on the impact on the conviction rate. Thus, the optimal
combination balances these two marginal effects, or rather,
[B(τq) −p q(τq)s + C]p q(τq) f (τq) = [p q(Tq)s −C −B(Tq)]p q(Tq) f (Tq).
The utility a prosecutor receives from acting to be retained and choosing to
forgo re-election is identical to (1) and (2) except τq and Tq are derived from
the previous expression achieving r e.
Hence, as before, the set of separating equilibria are those values of r e
that result in (1) exceeding (2) for q = H, but not q = L and the set of
pooling equilibria (assuming deviations are believed to come from q = L)
are those values of r greater thanrH where (1) exceeds (2) for both types.
In every separating equilibrium the high-quality prosecutor distorts her
handling of the caseload. Speciﬁcally, the range of cases taken to trial shift
toward those with a higher probability of success. In every pooling equilib-
rium the low-quality prosecutor (and also the high-quality one in many of the
outcomes) shifts the range of cases taken to trial toward, again, those cases
with a higher probability of success.
In the text, under the assumption of a single-crossing between the plea
bargain outcome and the expected sanction from trial, this shift leads to
fewer cases taken to trial relative to the ﬁrst-best outcome. With double-
crossing the decrease in trials is offset by an increase in trials with very high
probabilities of success. Now, the net effect depends on which shift is larger.
Thus, the result in the text continues to hold with this extension so long
as the decrease in the cases taken to trial, F (τq) −F (θq) exceeds the increase
in the number of cases taken to trial F (Tq) −F (θq).
