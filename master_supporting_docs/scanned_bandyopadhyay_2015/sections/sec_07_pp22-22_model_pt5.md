5. Continuum of Types
Rather than assume the incumbent prosecutor takes one of only two types
suppose, instead, there are a continuum of types. Let G (q) denote the dis-
tribution function over the support [qD, qU ]. Do the results previously iden-
tiﬁed continue to hold in this environment?
We assume that if replaced, the new prosecutor is someone chosen from
the above distribution. If replaced, let the expected value of voters in period
2 from the new prosecutor be given by EV (q). We know that there exists a
cutoff level of quality q, for which V (q) = EV (q). Given that, the median
voter wants to retain prosecutors with an identiﬁed quality at or above q,
while replacing those below. Using the previous notation, denote Xq and rq
as the ﬁrst-best levels of aggregate sanction and conviction rate, respectively,
that is achieved by q.
Under asymmetric information, the equilibria that arise result in a semi-
separation of the types. To elaborate, consider the use of aggregate sanction
as the metric to evaluate prosecutorial quality. Suppose the median voter re-
elects an incumbent prosecutor who is able to at least achieve an aggregate
sanction of X ′. Furthermore, he believes that a prosecutor who exceeds this
threshold is of a higher quality (and therefore is retained), while a prosecu-
tor who falls short is of lower quality (and is consequently replaced). Let q ′
denote the prosecutor who achieves X ′ in the ﬁrst-best outcome. It is straight-
forward to verify that a prosecutor with quality q > q ′ selects her ﬁrst-best
amount of prosecution. A prosecutor of a quality less than q ′ faces a trade-
off: she can distort her decision making by bringing X up to X ′ or she can
disregard retention and select the ﬁrst-best amount of prosecution. For those
with values of q close to q ′ it is best to distort up their decision to go to trial
to achieve X ′. For those with sufﬁciently low values of q such a choice is not
optimal. As a result, the equilibrium can be described as separation among
those prosecutors with very low values of q, pooling among those with inter-
mediate values of q, and separation (and retention) of those with very high
values of q.
The range of types that pool can be identiﬁed by using (6) from Section
3.1. Each type of prosecutor who ﬁnds the inequality presented in (6) to not
hold prefers to forgo retention. The type that ﬁnds the left-hand-side equal
to the right-hand-side is indifferent between the two choices. Denoting this
type as q ′′, it follows that a prosecutor whose quality falls within this range,
q ∈[q ′′, q ′] pools on the outcome X ′.20
