The results without type-dependent errors, εL = εH and ηL = ηH, then is
analogous to the work presented in the text since the existence of separating
equilibrium is unchanged. The interesting issue, then, is the impact of type-
dependent mistakes. A plausible environment to consider is one where there
is positive correlation between an individual prosecutor’s ability to win at trial
and that prosecutor’s ability to identify and prosecute truly guilty suspects.
Rather, εL > εH and ηL > ηH. It is this special case that we now consider.
As indicated earlier, the expected welfare from going to trial increases as
the probability of a mistake decreases. Thus, the value to a high-quality pros-
ecutor (holding constant the increased likelihood of conviction) of going to
trial exceeds that of a low-quality prosecutor. Adding to this the effect on the
likelihood of conviction, the effect is ampliﬁed. Graphically, it can be seen
in Figure C2 that the expected welfare from going to trial for a high-quality
prosecutor is an upward rotation in the “trial, low ε” curve. Similarly, as also
argued above, the value to plea bargaining is also greater for high-quality
prosecutors than low-quality prosecutors again due to the decreased likeli-
hood of mistake. Graphically, this would be an upward rotation in the “plea,
low η ”curve.
While one may be interested in the net effect of these two shifts (relative
to a low-quality prosecutor), the ﬁnal important issue to determine, though,
is whether the introduction of type-dependent errors affects the existence of
separating equilibrium. This is simply the question of whether one can guar-
antee that if a low-quality prosecutor is willing to take a case to trial, to ensure
her re-election, is it always the case that so too does a high-quality prosecu-
tor. Without type-dependent errors the answer is straightforward: yes. Be-
cause the high-quality prosecutor wins with a higher probability at trial, the
utility generated from going to trial always exceeds that of the low-quality
prosecutor.
With type-dependent errors, though, it is not as obvious. The reason is
that the high-quality prosecutor also ﬁnds the utility to plea bargaining to
be greater. Therefore, it is possible that if ηH is substantially less than ηL,
while εH is only modestly less than εL (and p H(θ) −p L(θ) is relatively small
as well), then there may be values of θ where EωH(trial) < EωH(plea),
while EωL(trial) > EωL(plea). Thus, the results in the text continue to hold
so long as ηL −ηH < T(εL −εH, p H −p L) where T(εL −εH, p H −p L) is a
threshold which depends on εL −εH and p H −p L.
