In the second column of Table 6 the various legal aid societies are collected into one
dummy variable LAS, as opposed to having a public defender or private representative. A
positive and significant coefficient on LAS indicates that when reelections are not going on,
those defendants represented by a legal aid society are more likely to have their conviction
upheld. The impact on such appellants during reelection is reversed (but statistically
insignificant). Thus, those represented by these organizations tend to experience more
accurate initial decisions, as compared to those who utilize a public defender (the omitted
variable), and be unaffected by prosecutor elections. The specification with the mode of the
conviction is not presented in Table 6. This is due to the fact that none of the interaction
terms are statistically significant. Thus, success in appeal both before and during a prosec-
utor's reelection is not driven by the way in which the conviction was obtained. This is an
important result. While previous research indicates a move toward more trials, anticipation
of the hawkishness may influence the plea bargaining (as suggested in Table 5). Hence, the
rate of convictions being upheld does not adjust during an election season.
Finally, the crimes committed are interacted with the reelection campaign. Three can
be shown to experience a change. Notice that the coefficient on all three interaction terms
is negative. Hence, the crimes of DWI, assault, and fraud/forgery all experience lower rates
of being upheld by the appellate court when the case is decided during a prosecutor's
reelection campaign. Also, the estimated marginal effects are quite large. The probability of
a conviction being upheld decreases by 40 percentage points for DWIs, while assault and
fraud convictions experience a decrease of 24 and 88 percentage points, respectively.
Although not reported, a dummy variable for whether a "serious" crime was committed is
created. This variable, along with an interaction term with REELECT, are statistically insig-
nificant. Thus, there is no systematic difference between the prosecution of serious and
nonserious crimes either in reelection season or out of it.
